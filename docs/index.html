About

I am a PhD candidate in the J. Mike Walker '66 Department of Mechanical Engineering at Texas A&M University, working with Prof. N. K. Anand in the Computational Heat Transfer Laboratory. My research sits at the intersection of scientific machine learning and computational transport phenomena: I build graph-based neural surrogates that respect conservation laws, learn from unstructured CFD meshes, and generalize across geometries and operating conditions, and I use them to accelerate design and analysis of thermal-fluid systems without giving up the reliability of first-principles solvers.

Before Texas A&M, I completed an MS in Mechanical Engineering at Georgia Southern University with Dr. Hayri Sezer in the Fire and Energy (FireGY) Laboratory, where I developed finite-volume models of coupled heat and mass transfer in metal hydride hydrogen storage reactors and a three-dimensional model of gypsum board dehydration under fire exposure that was validated against experiments and supported a National Institute of Justice-funded project. I am a first-generation college graduate, and mentoring students who are new to research is a central part of how I work.

Research

High-fidelity simulation of transport phenomena is accurate but prohibitively slow to repeat across the many geometries and operating conditions that design, optimization, and safety analysis demand. My research vision is to make physics-consistent learned models a trusted complement to numerical solvers: fast enough for design loops and digital twins, and constrained enough by conservation laws to be believed. I pursue this through three connected thrusts. All figures below are from my own papers and presentations.

Thrust 1

Geometry-aware, physics-regularized graph learning for thermal-fluid systems

I design graph neural network surrogates that operate directly on unstructured CFD meshes. The residual multilayer-perceptron graph convolutional network (ResMLP-GCN) published in Physics of Fluids (2025) takes Reynolds number and geometric descriptors as inputs and predicts coupled velocity fields for the double lid-driven cavity (trained Re 100–2000, tested to Re 3000) and backward-facing step flows, reproducing the dominant flow structures well beyond the training range. The follow-on physics-regularized surrogate, under review in Physics of Fluids, is trained on a single step height and predicts reattachment lengths and velocity fields for step heights varied by ±40%, the property needed for geometry optimization and rapid design screening.

  • Physics of Fluids 37, 107138 (2025) · SSRN preprint (under review)
  • Presented at WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026 (Munich) and ASME IMECE 2025 (Memphis)

Thrust 2

Multiphysics transport in reactive porous media for energy storage and fire safety

Using in-house finite-volume solvers, I model coupled heat, mass, and momentum transfer with reaction kinetics in porous media. For metal hydride hydrogen storage, I quantified how operating parameters and reactor geometry govern absorption and desorption performance (Int. J. Hydrogen Energy, 2024) and wrote a comprehensive review of the field (Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2026). For fire safety, I developed a three-dimensional dehydration and calcination model of gypsum board under arbitrary heat flux configurations, validated against cone-calorimeter measurements (Int. J. Thermal Sciences, 2026), that supports forensic fire investigation. Related work covers lithium-ion thermal runaway with decomposition kinetics (ASME IMECE 2023) and fire-induced injury in tree stems (accepted, Int. Commun. Heat Mass Transfer).

  • Int. J. Thermal Sciences 221, 110457 (2026)
  • Renew. Sustain. Energy Rev. 226, 116294 (2026)
  • Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 71, 1045–1055 (2024)

Thrust 3

Scalable, physics-informed PDE learning and hybrid solver-ML workflows

I work on the infrastructure that makes learned PDE models usable at scale. I developed a solver-free, method-of-manufactured-solutions data-generation framework for training and benchmarking neural operators across PDE classes, parameter ranges, and boundary conditions (ASME IMECE 2026, to appear), and I study stable autoregressive rollout of graph surrogates for transient advection-diffusion systems. As part of the ShockCast team, I contributed to a two-phase framework that learns adaptive time-step sizes for supersonic flows with shocks, published at ICLR 2026 with code in the open-source AIRS library. Earlier, I implemented physics-informed neural networks in Julia SciML for transient scalar transport and coupled learned models with OpenFOAM.

  • ICLR 2026 (OpenReview) · arXiv:2506.07969
  • AIRS library (ShockCast)

Selected projects

Geometry optimization with geometry-generalizable surrogates

A physics-regularized GNN trained on one backward-facing step geometry predicts reattachment lengths across step heights varied by ±40% and Reynolds numbers 125–375, mostly within 10% of OpenFOAM. This makes shape sweeps and geometry optimization loops feasible at a fraction of CFD cost.

Preprint (SSRN) · under review, Physics of Fluids; WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026 talk.

Parametric extrapolation with ResMLP-GCN

Error growth of the ResMLP-GCN surrogate for the double lid-driven cavity as Reynolds number moves from the training range (100–2000) into extrapolation (2000–3000). The surrogate degrades smoothly rather than failing abruptly, a prerequisite for trustworthy use in design.

Physics of Fluids 37, 107138 (2025)

Metal hydride hydrogen storage reactors

Coupled heat and mass transfer with absorption/desorption kinetics in a finned reactor, validated against experiments. The study quantifies how supply pressure, coolant temperature, and reactor dimensions set charging time, informing safer and faster hydrogen storage design.

Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 71 (2024) · IMECE 2023 · RSER review (2026)

Thermal runaway in lithium-ion batteries

A finite-volume model with four-step decomposition kinetics and inter-cell contact resistance reproduces oven-test critical ambient temperatures for single cells and four-cell ensembles to within 1 °C of experiment, matching or improving on COMSOL and GPYRO benchmarks.

ASME IMECE 2023; ESSCI 2024 and U.S. National Combustion Meeting 2023 presentations.

Future directions

  • Graph neural operators with built-in conservation. Discretization-aware architectures whose message passing mirrors finite-volume flux balances, so that learned surrogates conserve mass and energy by construction and can be embedded in geometry optimization loops.
  • Physics-informed digital twins for energy storage and thermal safety. Hybrid models for hydrogen storage reactors and battery packs that assimilate sparse sensor data and flag thermal-runaway precursors in real time.
  • Hybrid SciML-FVM solvers. Learned closures, preconditioners, and adaptive time-stepping coupled with OpenFOAM to accelerate, rather than replace, trusted CFD workflows.

Teaching and Mentoring

I teach the way I was taught best: by meeting students where they are, connecting every derivation to a physical consequence they can picture, and building a classroom in which asking a basic question is normal. My goal is for students to leave a course able to set up a problem from first principles, choose a numerical method deliberately, and check whether the answer makes physical sense.
