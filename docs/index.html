About

Muhammad Hasnain is a PhD researcher in Mechanical Engineering at Texas A&M University working at the intersection of scientific machine learning, computational fluid dynamics, and multiphysics transport modeling. His current doctoral work focuses on graph-based, geometry-aware surrogate frameworks for thermal-fluid systems and high-fidelity CFD acceleration.

He previously completed an MS in Mechanical Engineering at Georgia Southern University, where his thesis developed coupled heat and mass transfer models for metal-hydride hydrogen storage systems. Across his MS and PhD trajectory, he has built in-house finite-volume solvers, integrated OpenFOAM with machine-learning workflows, and developed graph/CNN surrogates with strong geometric and parametric generalization.

Research Experience and Technical Contributions

My work integrates scientific machine learning, high-fidelity numerical methods, and applied thermal-fluid engineering to build robust, interpretable, and deployment-ready computational models.

Aug 2024 - Present · Texas A&M University

Graduate Research Assistant, Computational Heat Transfer Laboratory

  • Designed and trained GNN architectures in PyTorch/PyTorch Geometric for spatiotemporal heat-transfer and fluid-flow prediction with strong generalization across unseen geometries and operating regimes.
  • Built supervised and semi-supervised pipelines for transient advection-diffusion learning, improving autoregressive forecast stability for long-horizon prediction and failure-mode tracking.
  • Contributed to a two-phase deep-learning framework for adaptive time-stepping in high-speed flow; this work was published at ICLR 2026 and targets scalable real-time simulation.
  • Developed physics-regularized GNN surrogates with custom loss design for geometry-generalizable CFD prediction (under review, Journal of Computational Physics).
  • Designed solver-free MMS data-generation pipelines for systematic benchmarking across PDE classes, parameter ranges, and boundary-condition configurations.

Aug 2022 - Jul 2024 · Georgia Southern University

Graduate Research Assistant, Fire and Energy (FireGY) Lab

  • Implemented PINNs in Julia SciML and in-house finite-volume solvers in Python/MATLAB; repeatedly validated numerical outputs against experimental datasets.
  • Built coupled heat, mass, and momentum transfer models in reactive porous media for hydrogen storage, energy conversion, and fire safety applications.
  • Developed the first 3D numerical model for gypsum-board dehydration under fire exposure and validated results against TGA-informed benchmarks.
  • Analyzed multivariable thermal-runaway signatures in Li-ion battery models to identify dominant failure pathways and improve mechanistic interpretation.
  • Extended modeling workflows to additional transport-driven systems including tree mortality and thermal injury prediction tasks.
