About

I am a PhD candidate in the J. Mike Walker '66 Department of Mechanical Engineering at Texas A&M University, working with Prof. N. K. Anand in the Computational Heat Transfer Laboratory. My research sits at the intersection of scientific machine learning and computational transport phenomena: I build graph-based neural surrogates that respect conservation laws, learn from unstructured CFD meshes, and generalize across geometries and operating conditions, and I use them to accelerate design and analysis of thermal-fluid systems without giving up the reliability of first-principles solvers.

Before Texas A&M, I completed an MS in Mechanical Engineering at Georgia Southern University with Dr. Hayri Sezer in the Fire and Energy (FireGY) Laboratory, where I developed finite-volume models of coupled heat and mass transfer in metal hydride hydrogen storage reactors and fire-response models of gypsum board and tree stems that were validated against experiments and supported a National Institute of Justice-funded project. I am a first-generation college graduate, and mentoring students who are new to research is a central part of how I work.

Research

High-fidelity simulation of transport phenomena is accurate but too slow to repeat across the many geometries and operating conditions that design, optimization, and safety analysis demand. My work makes physics-consistent learned models a trusted complement to numerical solvers, and applies validated finite-volume multiphysics models to fire safety and energy storage problems. It is organized in four areas; all figures below are taken from my own papers and presentations, and each opens at full size when clicked.

Area 1

Geometry-aware, physics-regularized graph learning for thermal-fluid systems

I designed graph neural network surrogates that operate directly on unstructured CFD meshes. The residual multilayer-perceptron graph convolutional network (ResMLP-GCN), published in Physics of Fluids (2025), takes Reynolds number and geometric descriptors as inputs and predicts coupled velocity fields for the double lid-driven cavity and backward-facing step. Trained on Re 100–2000, it reproduced the dominant flow structures at Re 3000, well beyond the training range. I then developed a physics-regularized surrogate, now under review in Physics of Fluids, that was trained on a single step height and predicted reattachment lengths and velocity fields for step heights varied by ±40%, the property required for geometry optimization and rapid design screening. I presented this work at WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026 and ASME IMECE 2025.

  • Physics of Fluids 37, 107138 (2025) · SSRN preprint (under review)

Area 2

Fire safety: thermal response of building materials and vegetation

In the Fire and Energy Laboratory at Georgia Southern, I built finite-volume models of coupled heat and mass transfer with reaction kinetics in porous solids exposed to fire. For gypsum board, the most common wall lining, I developed a three-dimensional dehydration and calcination model under arbitrary heat-flux configurations, validated it against cone-calorimeter measurements at four depths, and showed how calcination depth can be related to the heat-flux history a wall experienced, which gives fire investigators a quantitative tool (Int. J. Thermal Sciences, 2026; project funded by the National Institute of Justice). For vegetation, I co-developed a predictive heat transfer model of tree stems under surface fire that resolves bark and wood layers with radially varying moisture, matched Douglas-fir experiments more closely than FireStem2D, and converted the temperature history into cell-death rate and necrotic depth for symmetric and one-sided exposures and for several species and moisture contents (Int. Commun. Heat Mass Transfer, 2026).

  • Int. Commun. Heat Mass Transfer 180, 112653 (2026)
  • Int. J. Thermal Sciences 221, 110457 (2026)

Area 3

Energy storage and conversion systems: hydrogen storage and lithium-ion safety

For metal hydride hydrogen storage, I formulated and solved coupled heat, mass, and momentum transfer with absorption and desorption kinetics in finned and jacketed reactors, validated the model against equilibrium-pressure, reacted-fraction, and temperature measurements from three independent experiments, and quantified how supply pressure, coolant temperature, and reactor dimensions set charging time (Int. J. Hydrogen Energy, 2024; MS thesis). I then wrote a comprehensive review that consolidates two decades of reactor-scale modeling and identifies the open problems in coupled kinetics, heat management, and reactor geometry (Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2026). For lithium-ion batteries, I co-developed a finite-volume thermal runaway model with four-step decomposition kinetics and inter-cell contact resistance that reproduced oven-test critical ambient temperatures for single cells and four-cell ensembles to within 1 °C of experiment and extended it to a 16-cell module (ASME IMECE 2023).

  • Renew. Sustain. Energy Rev. 226, 116294 (2026)
  • Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 71, 1045–1055 (2024) · IMECE2023-113787

Area 4

Scalable, physics-informed PDE learning and hybrid solver-ML workflows

I work on the infrastructure that makes learned PDE models usable at scale. I developed a solver-free, method-of-manufactured-solutions data-generation framework for training and benchmarking neural operators across PDE classes, parameter ranges, and boundary conditions (ASME IMECE 2026, to appear), and I studied stable autoregressive rollout of graph surrogates for transient advection-diffusion systems. As part of the ShockCast team, I contributed to a two-phase framework that learns adaptive time-step sizes for supersonic flows with shocks, published at ICLR 2026 with code in the open-source AIRS library. Earlier, I implemented physics-informed neural networks in Julia SciML for transient scalar transport and coupled learned models with OpenFOAM.

  • ICLR 2026 (OpenReview) · arXiv:2506.07969
  • AIRS library (ShockCast)

Selected projects

Fire-induced injury and mortality in tree stems

A two-dimensional heat transfer model of a tree stem with bark and wood layers, radially varying moisture, and a measured surface-fire heat flux. Temperature evolution under one-sided exposure (shown at 400–1140 s) was converted into tissue death rate and necrotic depth, 9.5 mm for uniform exposure and 4.4 mm for one-sided exposure, and compared across species and moisture contents to support prescribed-burn planning and post-fire mortality assessment.

Int. Commun. Heat Mass Transfer 180, 112653 (2026) · ESSCI 2024 presentation.

Geometry optimization with geometry-generalizable surrogates

A physics-regularized GNN trained on one backward-facing step geometry predicted reattachment lengths across step heights varied by ±40% and Reynolds numbers 125–375, mostly within 10% of OpenFOAM, making shape sweeps and geometry optimization loops feasible at a fraction of CFD cost.

Preprint (SSRN) · under review, Physics of Fluids; WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026 talk.

Gypsum board under non-uniform fire exposure

The three-dimensional dehydration and calcination model applied to a Gaussian heat-flux footprint on a 12 mm board. Temperature histories at the exposed, middle, and unexposed surfaces show how localized exposure produces steep in-plane gradients, the link between observed calcination patterns and the location and intensity of the exposure.

Int. J. Thermal Sciences 221, 110457 (2026) · Top Eagle Award poster, 2024.

Parametric extrapolation with ResMLP-GCN

Error growth of the ResMLP-GCN surrogate for the double lid-driven cavity as Reynolds number moves from the training range (100–2000) into extrapolation (2000–3000). The surrogate degraded smoothly rather than failing abruptly, a prerequisite for trustworthy use in design.

Physics of Fluids 37, 107138 (2025)

Metal hydride hydrogen storage reactors

Reacted-fraction and temperature fields during charging of a finned reactor, with parametric sweeps over supply pressure and coolant temperature. The study quantified how these operating parameters and the reactor dimensions set charging time, informing safer and faster hydrogen storage design.

Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 71 (2024) · IMECE 2023 · RSER review (2026)

Thermal runaway in lithium-ion batteries

A finite-volume model with four-step decomposition kinetics and inter-cell contact resistance reproduced oven-test critical ambient temperatures for single cells and four-cell ensembles to within 1 °C of experiment, matching or improving on COMSOL and GPYRO benchmarks, and predicted the onset time and critical temperature of a 16-cell module.

ASME IMECE 2023 · ESSCI 2024 and U.S. National Combustion Meeting 2023 presentations.

Future directions

  • Graph neural operators with built-in conservation. Discretization-aware architectures whose message passing mirrors finite-volume flux balances, so that learned surrogates conserve mass and energy by construction and can be embedded in geometry optimization loops.
  • Physics-informed digital twins for energy storage and thermal safety. Hybrid models for hydrogen storage reactors and battery packs that assimilate sparse sensor data and flag thermal-runaway precursors in real time.
  • Hybrid SciML-FVM solvers. Learned closures, preconditioners, and adaptive time-stepping coupled with OpenFOAM to accelerate, rather than replace, trusted CFD workflows.

Teaching and Mentoring

I teach the way I was taught best: by meeting students where they are, connecting every derivation to a physical consequence they can picture, and building a classroom in which asking a basic question is normal. My goal is for students to leave a course able to set up a problem from first principles, choose a numerical method deliberately, and check whether the answer makes physical sense.
